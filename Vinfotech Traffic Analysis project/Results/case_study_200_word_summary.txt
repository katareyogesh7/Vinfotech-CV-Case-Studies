A fixed elevated video of a signalised intersection was analysed with yolo11s.pt for vehicle detection and BoT-SORT for identity association. To balance inference cost against temporal resolution, frames were processed at approximately 15.0 frames per second while all events retained original video timestamps. The pipeline captured bounding boxes, confidence scores, classes, and vehicle contact points for offline analysis. Entry and exit counting used manually defined directional gates. A 4×4 movement matrix was calculated only when an entering vehicle and a later outgoing crossing shared a track ID. The program identified 261 candidate completed turning movements, while 71 incoming gate events remained unmatched. Unmatched movements were not assigned destinations. Stationary-vehicle episodes were estimated using smoothed contact-point speeds inside four approach regions, producing queue proxies and available waiting-time samples. Results were exported as CSV, JSON, figures and an annotated video. Important limitations include partial occlusion of one approach, small distant vehicles, compression artefacts, and ID fragmentation. Gate positions and counting samples require manual video verification before final accuracy claims. Source note: Archived Bellevue intersection video dated September 11, 2017; not newly captured live footage.. The reported values are provisional computer-vision estimates rather than certified ground-truth traffic counts.
